## WHAT

Stopped a live resolve accidentally triggered by the lead's Miller code-intelligence call, preserved forensic state, and repaired the orphaned coordinator claim.

## WHY

The new Miller server became leader and launched request `3214cfe1b08f4f608934b0ae9e7e08db` against the live family store. This violated the baseline's isolation boundary and left a dead-owner `claimed` resolve after the harness-owned server/child were terminated.

## HOW

- Confirmed the assigned one-file Luna had run no harness command; the live process came from the lead MCP call.
- Terminated only new harness-owned PIDs `389543` and `391671`; ordinary Miller reader PIDs were untouched.
- Verified no writer lease and no generation promotion; `CURRENT` and the source pointer hashes stayed unchanged.
- Ran read-only `store maintain repair` planning; it reported valid roots and no recovery action for the claim.
- Backed up live `coord.db` to `~/.miller/perf-recovery-task1b-b-baseline-c9c57645/live-coord-before-requeue-3214cfe1.db` (SHA-256 `36aec9353c515ffa89b588df2e82806f89b97a69f583c02fb9c04ad9ea19602a`).
- In one conditional transaction, requeued only the exact dead-owner resolve and cleared its claim fields, matching the tested coordinator recovery transition. Preserved scratch files for normal recovery.

## IMPACT

The live family is no longer blocked by a stranded claimed resolve. The incident also proves that an apparently read-only Miller tool connection can trigger a long live leader convergence/resolve, which is material Task 5 evidence. Further baseline work must avoid Miller MCP calls until the live leader admission defect is characterized.
