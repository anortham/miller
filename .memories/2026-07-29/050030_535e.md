## The decisive experiment ran: Miller 2.2x over a bare agent, strict dominance, budget-proof

**WHAT** — All four approved calibration runs completed and scored on 2026-07-28/29 (~4h wall, user-approved Codex spend). Finding: `docs/findings/2026-07-29-miller-vs-bare-agent-calibration.md`; exports committed under `docs/findings/agent-efficiency/2026-07-29-bare-agent/` (identities c880c196/a06988d7/f87380da/3ae25ec3).

**Results** —
- A (bare vs Miller v1.14.0 archive): correct 5→11 /15 frozen, 4→10 raised; wrong-action 26.7%→0% frozen; recall@6 28.2%→67.9%; `baseline_only=0` in every run (strict dominance). Doubling the bare agent's budget made it WORSE (5→4) — kills the strangled-baseline objection.
- Product-neutral 10-task subset (5 tasks are exact-symbol-identity, bare solved ZERO ever): bare 5/10 vs Miller-on 7/10 frozen. ~Half of Miller's win is the exact-identity capability class, half retrieval+discipline.
- Unconditional anchor recall much closer (0.59-0.66 vs 0.73-0.81): the bare agent finds evidence but mis-acts — Miller's edge is identity precision + act-on-evidence discipline, not just search.
- B (off vs on): lexical core carries the product (11/15); semantic adds +1..2 correct, +8..13pts recall, never loses a task.
- Efficiency gate honestly FAILS: Miller spends more per task than a failing-fast baseline.

**Decision (per brief step 2 rule)** — Delta is decisive ⇒ this becomes the marketing story for the distribution month (Step 3). Honest frame: exact identity + bounded-context discipline, at higher per-task cost.
