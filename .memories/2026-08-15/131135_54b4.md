## WHAT

Completed the enabled-only candidate-query timing replay for the remaining full-resolution Linux gate.

- Measured full request: 148,431 ms external wall, 148,034 ms CPU, 26,867,712-byte peak PSS, exit 0.
- Internal profile: 148,405 ms wall, 148,391 ms accounted, 14 ms residual (0.0094%).
- Resolution: 139,497 ms; identifiers: 111,058 ms; pending: 28,083 ms.
- `children_named`: 79,507,575 µs across 715,409 executions returning 234,672 rows.
- All candidate query families total 130,009,293 µs, 93.2% of resolution.

## WHY

The plan forbids optimizing the broad resolver until the real production-volume snapshot identifies the dominant subphase and query family. Aggregate counts alone proved amplification but could not identify which family owned wall time.

## HOW

A temporary default-off profiler enabled session-local clocks only for profiled base-build/exact sessions and timed all 14 existing query families. One fresh copied-store replay used the exact production `producer.resolve.full` path. The staged pointer restored automatically to SHA-256 `7cdd3ba66ebabb0a5994a9d75dfcbd458b20547506c8a278d658e4bf5842bce6`; no producer process remains.

## IMPACT

The remaining defect is now proven as a database N+1 dominated by `children_named`, not startup, locking, finalization, or memory. The smallest justified production slice is to batch or reuse this lookup while preserving exact full/scoped digests and keeping the full resolver oracle.
