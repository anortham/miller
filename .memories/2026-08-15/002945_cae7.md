## WHAT

Completed the first strict quiet-Linux recovery replay on a verified fresh offline family-store copy and isolated two independent remaining bottlenecks.

## WHY

The recovery plan requires end-to-end evidence, not synthetic success. The strict replay is the first exact production-contract measurement after Tasks 1–7B.

## HOW

- Replayed 14 workloads with 12 warmups and 42 measured rows from `/home/murphy/.miller/task8-final-quiet2.NAUEb2/store`.
- Verified parity, semantic, truncation, memory, pointer restoration, and protected-process safety.
- Found every semantic-off read tool pays a common ~6.8-second floor with ~1.82M reads, ~801k writes, ~147 MB writes, and ~383 MB PSS.
- Static tracing identified `FamilyStoreReadSession.CreateCompatibilityProjection` as the leading common owner; exact phase attribution is now delegated against a disposable snapshot.
- Found `producer.resolve.one_file` times out at 60 seconds in all three rows. The public forced-full control took 149.348 seconds, with 144.440 seconds in resolution. A bounded long one-file observation is required to distinguish full-crossover selection from a scoped-path regression.
- Corrected a diagnostic safety failure: a worker initially profiled from the wrong cwd and opened the live family store read-only. The process was stopped; a corrected staged-cwd run proved no live-store access and matched the strict ledger digest. No live producer or protected process was signaled.

## IMPACT

The replay infrastructure is functioning and most recovery gates pass, but Task 8 cannot close until the shared read-session cost and one-file resolver mode are proven and fixed. Windows validation remains deferred until the Linux fixes pass.
