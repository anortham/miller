## WHAT

## WHY

The indexed-store replay is still 98.667 seconds. Children and broad-name prime own 44.178 seconds, so another small cache or index change cannot close the 60-second gate.

## HOW

- Proved broad-name prime saturates in 1,565/1,668 windows, skips 124,556/190,697 names, and fills the shared 2W cache before exact-child admission.
- Proved 161,446/176,850 child executions are tier1 terminal, tier3 receiver, or receiver-shadow ancestor shapes that become knowable after bounded parent discovery.
- Rejected prime expansion, additive ancestor prefetch, JSON/temp-table transport, cache growth, and recursive wide joins.
- Selected a TDD slice that replaces broad-name priming only for scope-bearing phases with nearest-depth exact child-key batches, ordinary VALUES parameters, existing count/fetch admission, unchanged W/2W/3W bounds, and scalar fallback.
- Grok estimated 57–64 seconds; Sol treats this as uncertain because top-level/filtered SQL may absorb displaced name-prime work. One exact replay decides retention.
