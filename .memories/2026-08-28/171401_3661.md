## Audited machine-wide Miller telemetry after the 1.25.0 rebuild

### WHAT

Reviewed all rows in `/home/murphy/.miller/telemetry.db` from August 2 through August 28, grouped calls by release/build, day, workspace, tool, operation, outcome, and exact stored error. Compared same-workspace latency cohorts and inspected slow-call phase telemetry.

### WHY

Many releases landed in two weeks. Lifetime totals and mixed workloads can make current behavior look worse than it is, so conclusions must separate historical failures from current release behavior.

### HOW

- Verified SQLite integrity, 47,174-row export parity, and zero dropped writes.
- Error rate fell from 11.1% on 1.19.1 and 9.9% on 1.19.2 to 0.057% across 10,608 calls on 1.24.2. The first 1.25.0 calls have no errors.
- Most retained errors are historical: 1,028 stale search-sidecar errors, 233 stale content-sidecar errors, and 107 family-generation lazy-load races.
- A temporary 1.24.2 performance regression came from `workspace_refresh` waits. On the Miller workspace, 584 affected search calls averaged 1.69 seconds versus 254 ms for pinned index-load calls; later builds removed the observed wait.
- Current 1.25.0 has too few calls for a trend. Initial calls show search at 26 to 304 ms and context at 4.5 seconds.
- Remaining candidates are cold edit previews around 4.6 to 5.3 seconds p95, context/impact graph lookup tails, two August 27 FTS SQLite lock errors, and misleading health wording.

### IMPACT

Future audits should compare release/build cohorts and `wait_reason`, not lifetime averages. `workspace health` currently calls `SummarizeOutcomes` without a time predicate but labels the retained count as "recent", so old fixed failures keep the workspace warning active.
