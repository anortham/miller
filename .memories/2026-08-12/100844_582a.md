## WHAT

Added a request-local exact Search result cache to ContextTool.

- Keys are the exact `(query, limit, mode)` tuple.
- At most 64 keys and 10,000 returned-hit references are retained for one synchronous context request.
- Cache hits return the same result object/order and are surfaced as a fixed `CacheHit` telemetry family with zero backend elapsed time.

## WHY

The correlation-bound diagnostic proved that anchor resolution reissued eight exact Search requests already completed during term retrieval, costing 325 ms. Source tracing showed this is the intentional test-subject rescue pass admitting tests after the initial hidden-test term search.

## HOW

ContextTool creates one internal lookup wrapper and resolver after resolving the pinned workspace context, then routes semantic, source, actionable, and reference-aware work through that same wrapper. The shared generation-cached measured index remains cache-free. Query, mode, or limit changes miss. Requests/results beyond either fixed cap execute normally and are not cached.

A first implementation used only a 64-entry cap; lead review rejected it because 64 escalation-sized result lists could retain hundreds of thousands of hits. Production-shaped oversized and cumulative-budget REDs now enforce the second row cap.

## IMPACT

The eight proven duplicate disk rankings should disappear without changing search ranking, filtering, generation identity, public APIs, or persisted state. Search telemetry continues to distinguish actual backend executions from avoided cache hits.
