## Whole-stack architecture assessment: materialized global resolution is the structural cost

**WHAT:** Full-stack perf review of Miller + julie-extract + julie-semantic-sidecar, written to `docs/findings/2026-08-18-whole-stack-architecture-assessment.md`. No code changed.

**WHY:** After the two-week Aug 2026 perf campaign the user asked whether the stack is fundamentally doing something wrong at a higher level.

**HOW:** Three parallel exploration agents (Miller pipeline map, julie store/resolution design, recorded perf numbers) plus live measurements on this machine (CLI timings, log phase distributions, store.db dbstat).

**Key evidence:**
- Reads fast: search 0.22 s, inspect 0.19 s cold-process. Extraction fast: ~3.4 ms/file, no-change rescan 51 ms.
- Resolve phase: p95 60–77 s, max 250–600 s, 117 min/day on this small repo. One-file production resolve still 178.6 s vs 5 s budget.
- Write amplification ~200x: 30 MB source → ~6+ GB derived (store.db 3.1 GB, 14 bases ~1.5 GB, triplicate sidecars, 990 MB legacy symbols.db). Full manifest copy per revision (702 k rows).
- Ph0 cost model: one-file change re-derives 74.5% of corpus by construction; binding a base to a sibling tip is 32.4% SLOWER than rebuilding from scratch.
- Tier-4 unique-language-global binding makes resolution non-local by definition; scope planner does 4+ O(repo) passes before any work.
- ~330 GB of `~/.miller` perf-test scratch (task8*, perf-recovery-*) reclaimable, not deleted (needs approval).

**IMPACT / recommendation:** Store facts, resolve at query time (name→candidates + name→sites indexes make tier rules per-query lookups); shrink the family store to a content-addressed per-file fact cache, retiring coordinator/leases/generations/bases/deltas/rebases. Validation spike defined with parity + latency gates before adoption. In-place fallbacks ranked in the doc (persisted name→file index, cached read session, O(N²) import quantum fix, batched delta subprocesses, finer sidecar stamps, 30 s consumer deadline).
