## WHAT

Completed bounded warm throughput measurements against the live shared semantic broker after the Miller vector rebuild reached ready.

## WHY

The earlier trace established full regeneration and concurrent broker use but did not separate short-input throughput from the cost of real symbol cards and long content chunks.

## HOW

- Used the existing `Miller.SemanticBrokerProbe` with batch size 64 plus one query, interval 0. After a discarded 2-second warmup, three 3-second runs delivered 343.0–346.0 short texts/s with zero failures, hangs, or reconnects.
- Used a temporary helper outside the repository that calls the same public `SemanticEmbeddingSession` and `SharedSemanticBrokerConnectionFactory` against the same endpoint/model.
- Sampled 64 deterministic evenly spaced current-store symbol cards built by `SymbolCardBuilder.Build`. Exact lengths: 142–620 chars, p50 241, p95 496; 7–58 whitespace words, p50 10, p95 54. Warmup was 1686.508 ms; measured batches were 1644.905, 1659.012, and 1643.463 ms, or 38.6–38.9 cards/s.
- Sampled 64 deterministic evenly spaced content chunks transformed by `SymbolCardBuilder.ChunkText`. Exact lengths: 217–4000 chars, p50 3991, p95 3999; 9–554 whitespace words, p50 343, p95 532. Warmup was 12606.958 ms; measured batches were 12778.625, 12699.315, and 12656.917 ms, or 5.01–5.06 chunks/s.
- The live rebuild embedded 1,999 chunks in 388.348 s (5.15 chunks/s) and 27,003 symbol cards in 625.427 s (43 cards/s), closely matching the representative warm batch rates.

## IMPACT

The delay was dominated by expected model inference cost for the actual long chunk and card inputs during a full regeneration. Shared broker contention existed, but the matched warm measurements show it is not needed to explain the observed rebuild throughput. The short toy probe was fast because its inputs were only 13–20 characters.
